### What is a staff engineer

A staff engineer is someone who feels accountable for the big picture.

### Pillars of the Technical Career Path

Big picture thinking, project execution, and leveling up (yourself and others) form the foundation for an impactful career in technology.

### The Four Core Disciplines

The book quotes Yonatan Zunger from Twigger who identifies four disciplines needed in any job: core technical skills, product management (what are we building and why), project management (day-to-day practicalities of completing tasks), and people management. 

### Archetypes of a Staff Engineer

A staff engineer typically falls into one of four archetypes: tech lead, architect, solver, or right hand.

### Three Mental Maps

To succeed in any company, you need to draw three mental maps:

1. **Locator Map** - Understand where you are within the organization.
2. **Topographical Map** - Recognize the landscape, including challenges and opportunities.
3. **Treasure Map** - Identify where the value and key contributions lie.

### Practical Advice for Staff Engineers

- **Boring Ideas Win**: In technology, the best ideas are often the simplest and most straightforward.
- **Join Existing Expeditions**: It's usually better to join ongoing projects rather than starting from scratch.
- **Get a sponsor:** as someone who is expected to use influence and persuasion to succeed, it's important to identify a sponsor for your work to make sure it gains traction.
- **Don't work alone**: Especially when developing a technical strategy, establish a core team and get early and frequent feedback.
- **Anticipate future needs:** Reflect on what you wish you had done.
- **Tidy:** Keep things clean and easy to pick up for the next person to pick up this work.
- **Institutional Knowledge:** Document in a way that assumes there will be staff turnover.
- **Expect Failure:** Plan for unexpected setbacks and failures from the beginning.
- **Optimize for maintenance:** Don't focus soley on creation.

### Prioritize your Time 

Time is a finite resource and there will be more things demanding your attention than you have time for. Consider the following factors to prioritize effectively:

- Energy (some project drain your energy and others restore it)
- Credibility
- Quality of life
- Skills development
- Social capital

### Making an Idea Stick

No matter how great your idea is, it needs to be easily understood to gain traction. Use memorable names for projects and create easy mental models to facilitate understanding.

### Current Role Self-Assessment

In order to know when it's time to change roles you should continuously be asking yourself these questions:

- Am I learning?
- Am I learning transferrable skills?
- Would I recruit friends to this work?
- Does this work make me feel confident and capable?
- Is this job good for my physical health, or is it causing stress?

### Engineer to Manager Path

- The best managers are often those who have recently transitioned from an engineering role (less than two years out).
- The best engineers have experience in product management.
- Having a hybrid role is generally not recommended. Instead, some professionals alternate between engineering and product management roles every two years to broaden their skill set.
